**What is the most interesting topic covered in your domain this quarter?**

The most interesting topic to me is how AI has become a common way for people to ask questions and learn about a place before visiting it. In our project, I find it especially interesting that we can use sentiment analysis to study how AI assistants describe a fertility clinic. However, the results depend heavily on the questions we ask. AI responses often sound neutral or positive, so general questions might not reveal patients’ concerns or negative experiences. Asking questions such as “What concerns have patients raised about this clinic?” could help us explore those perspectives. We would still need to check the original reviews to determine whether the responses accurately represent what patients think.

**Describe a potential investigation you would like to pursue for your Quarter 2 Project.**

I would like to investigate where prospective patients encounter difficulties after discovering RPSD, from their first inquiry to attending an initial consultation. Potential barriers include unclear costs, uncertainty about insurance coverage, scheduling delays, and confusion about next steps. Depending on data availability, we could analyze de-identified inquiry and scheduling records alongside patient surveys to identify where people delay or discontinue the process. Based on our findings, we could develop a prototype patient onboarding guide that explains the process and helps patients prepare for their first appointment. We could evaluate it through usability testing to see whether it helps people find information and understand what to do next.

**What is a potential change you’d make to the approach taken in your current Quarter 1 Project?**

I would expand our current questions to include both neutral questions and questions that explicitly ask about patient concerns or negative experiences. For example, we could compare “What do patients say about RPSD?” with “What complaints or concerns have patients reported about RPSD?” This would help us examine how question wording affects sentiment and whether the answers provide useful information for someone considering the clinic. We would also compare the responses with their cited reviews to check whether they present patient experiences fairly or emphasize positive or negative feedback disproportionately.

**What other techniques would you be interested in using in your project?**

I would be interested in using aspect-based sentiment analysis to understand patients’ opinions about specific parts of their experience, such as communication, costs, scheduling, and interactions with staff. An overall sentiment score could hide important differences between these areas. I would also like to use patient journey mapping and thematic analysis of surveys or interviews to understand where patients experience confusion or frustration. These techniques could help us identify practical improvements to the onboarding process and guide the design of a patient resource that we could evaluate through usability testing.
